One habit: Pause · Think · Act

  1. Pause. Stop before you reply, click or pay. Pressure is a warning sign.

  2. Think. Who is really asking, and what do they want from you?

  3. Act. Check another way, say no, or tell someone you trust.

The app can change. Pressure patterns stay the same.

Option B: Habit first

One habit for the moment something online feels wrong: Pause · Think · Act.

Did something already happen? You don't have to face this alone. Help is available right now. Get help

Here is the habit on a text many people get.

  1. Pause. Stop before you tap or pay. "Today" is hurry, and hurry is a sign of pressure.

  2. Think. Who is really asking? Were you even expecting a parcel?

  3. Act. Check another way. Don't tap the link. Look it up on the delivery company's real website, or where you bought the item.

The same three steps fit a game chat, a phone call or a new follower.

Where this comes from

ReadyOnline started by asking. In 2026 its founder surveyed 80 young people aged 14 to 22, and 153 adults. It's a small survey, not a national one.

Of the 80 young people, 80% said that knowing they won't get in trouble would help them ask for help. So this site starts from one rule: no blame. Something happening online is never a reason to go through it alone.
