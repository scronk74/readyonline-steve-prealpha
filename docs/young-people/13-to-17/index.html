Pressure, fake accounts and threats: how to spot them and get out.

You know the apps. Most of what happens there is fine. This page is about the moment a chat, a DM or a game stops being fun and starts being a push.

Did something already happen? It's not your fault, and you don't have to fix it alone. Go straight to Get help.

Three signs it's pressure

  • Hurry. "Do it now or you'll miss out." A real offer gives you time to check.

  • Secrecy. "Don't tell anyone." Secrecy is a red flag, even when someone is kind.

  • Owing. "After everything I gave you." A gift never means you owe someone access. A streak is not a promise you owe someone.

One sign is enough to pause. The app can change. Pressure patterns stay the same.

Pause · Think · Act

Pause. You don't have to answer right now. You do not need proof before you pause.

Think. Who is really asking? What do they want: a photo, a password, a code, money? A new account can look polished and still be false. Voices, photos and videos can be faked too.

Act. Pick a move:

  • Stop replying. You can leave without explaining yourself.

  • Check another way. Message the real person, or go to the real site yourself.

  • Block and report the account.

  • Tell someone you trust.

Someone you trust

Pick someone who stays calm and listens first. It doesn't have to be a parent. A relative, a teacher, a coach or an older brother or sister all count.

Ways to start:

  • "Can I show you something on my phone? I'm not sure about it."

  • "Something happened online. Can you just listen first?"

If the first person doesn't help, try someone else, or go to Get help.

When it's a friend

A friend may tell you before they tell any adult. You don't have to fix it.

  • Say: "This is not your fault. Let's find someone calm to help."

  • Don't promise to keep it secret. Try: "I won't tell everyone. But we need one person who can help."

In ReadyOnline's 2026 survey of 80 people aged 14 to 22, 76% said knowing they won't be judged would help them ask for help. You can be the person who doesn't judge.

See it happen: the free skin

A made-up chat from a game. Hints point out each sign as it shows up. Two to three minutes.

  1. A nice message.

    Kai_plays, someone you met in a match last week, writes: "gg! you're actually good. i've got a spare skin code. want it?"

    Hint: Nothing is wrong yet. Just notice who this is: someone you only know from the game.

    What this shows: Kind is fine. Kind doesn't tell you who someone is.

  2. A move, and a secret.

    Kai_plays: "dm me on the other app, it's easier. don't say anything in team chat, the others will want one too"

    Hint: Two things just changed. Where does Kai want to talk? Who isn't supposed to know?

    What this shows: Secrecy is a red flag, even when someone is kind. A private chat takes away the people who could help you notice pressure.

  3. A clock, and an ask.

    Kai_plays: "need your login for 2 min to add the skin. quick, the code runs out in 10"

    Hint: Who set the 10-minute clock? And why would a gift need your login?

    What this shows: Hurry is a sign. A real offer gives you time to check. A real gift doesn't need your password.

  4. Pause.

    You stop typing. You don't have to answer right now.

    Hint: Count the signs so far: secrecy and hurry. One is enough to pause, and you have two.

    What this shows: You do not need proof before you pause.

  5. Think.

    Who is really asking, and what do they want? Kai would get your account. You might get nothing.

    Hint: If the skin were real, Kai could send you the code and you could type it in yourself.

    What this shows: Thinking takes ten seconds. Ask who gains.

  6. Act.

    Your moves: stop replying, check another way, block and report, or tell someone you trust. You write "no thanks" and stay in team chat.

    Hint: Any of these moves works. Pick the one you can do right now.

    What this shows: You choose the move. You can leave without explaining yourself.

  7. The push back.

    Kai_plays: "wow. i was trying to be nice. thought we were friends"

    Hint: That's the third sign. Kai gave you something, and now says you owe something.

    What this shows: A gift never means you owe someone access. A push after you say no is one more reason to go.

  8. Out. And if you had already sent it?

    You block and report Kai_plays. Later you tell your cousin what happened.

    Hint: If you had sent your login, you would change your password and tell someone you trust. It would not be your fault.

    What this shows: Three signs: hurry, secrecy, owing. One habit: Pause · Think · Act.

If it's already gone further

Maybe you replied, clicked, sent something or paid. You can still get out. Someone used pressure or a trick on you. That's on them.

  • Shared a password or a login code? Change that password now.

  • Sent money? Go to Get help: "I think I've been scammed".

  • Someone is making threats? Go to Get help: "Someone wants images, or is threatening to share them".
