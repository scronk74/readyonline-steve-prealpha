Job offers, your accounts and your name online: how to check before you act.

You run your own accounts now: your bank, your job search, maybe a lease. Scams aimed at you rarely look like scams. They look like opportunities.

Did something already happen? It's not your fault, and you don't have to fix it alone. Go straight to Get help.

A job offer arrives fast

Pause. You didn't set that deadline. Hurry is a sign of pressure.

Think. Who is really asking, and what do they want? An honest employer doesn't need your password or a login code, and doesn't ask you to pay to get the job.

Act. Check another way. Find the company's official website yourself and ask there.

Real opportunities do not require rushed secrets. The same check works for a room to rent or a side income.

Protect your accounts

  • Start with your email. Your email can reset your other accounts. Give it a strong password that you use nowhere else.

  • Use a password manager. It creates and remembers a different password for every account.

  • Turn on two-step sign-in. You sign in with your password and then a second step, such as a code.

  • Never pass on a login code. A code is only for you. Anyone who asks you to send one or read it out is trying to get into your account.

Your name online

People may look you up: an employer, a landlord, someone you're about to work with.

  • Search your own name now and then, and see what they would see.

  • Choose who sees your posts, and close accounts you no longer use.

  • If someone copies your profile or posts as you, report the account in the app.

In ReadyOnline's 2026 survey of 80 people aged 14 to 22, 62% had seen a fake or copycat account, and 42% had been targeted by a money scam.

If it's already gone further

Someone used pressure or a trick on you. That's on them.

  • Shared a password or a login code? Change that password now, everywhere you use it.

  • Sent money, or card or bank details? Contact your bank or card company now, using the number on your card. Ask what they can do. Then go to Get help: "I think I've been scammed".

  • Someone is threatening to share a photo or video of you? That is blackmail, and it is not your fault. Don't pay, don't send more, and stop replying. Then go to Get help: "Someone wants images, or is threatening to share them".

Tell someone you trust. You don't have to explain everything first.

Need help now?

You don't have to face this alone. Help is available right now.
