Young people

Under 13, with a grown-up

Three steps for when something online feels wrong. Best read together. Reading on your own? That's okay. The three steps still work.

Did something already happen? It's not your fault. Tell a grown-up you trust, or go to Get help.

For the grown-up: the reading takes about two minutes. The talk can take as long as you like. One thing matters most: your child knows they can tell you about anything online, and that you will stay calm.

Read this together

Games, videos, chatting with friends: being online is fun. Sometimes something feels wrong. You don't have to work it out by yourself. Here is what to do.

  1. Pause. Stop. Don't tap. Don't type back.

  2. Think. Is someone rushing me? Do they want a secret? Are they asking for something of mine, like my password, my address or a picture of me?

  3. Act. Tell a grown-up you trust.

You can say: "Something online feels wrong. Can you help me?"

Talk about it

Take turns to answer.

  • What is your favourite thing to do online right now?

  • Has anything online ever felt weird or wrong?

  • Which two grown-ups could you tell?

Make a deal

Say it out loud.

Child: "If something online feels wrong, I'll tell you."

Grown-up: "If you tell me, I'll stay calm and listen first. You won't be in trouble for telling."

If you already tapped, replied or sent something

It's not your fault. People of every age get tricked online. Tell a grown-up you trust as soon as you can, even if you broke a rule. Telling is how you get help.

If that grown-up is busy or doesn't help, tell another one. Keep telling until someone helps.

Need help now?

You don't have to face this alone. Help is available right now.
